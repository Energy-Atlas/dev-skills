# Layouts

A small set of layouts on one grid. Every content slide uses one of the seven layouts below; the title slide, an optional
section divider, the references slide, and appendix slides follow the same grid.

## Grid (16:9, 13.333 x 7.5 in)

Defaults; adapt them once per deck (for example to a required template) and then keep them.

| Zone | Left | Top | Width | Height |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Margins | 0.6 in left and right | 0.45 in top | | 0.45 in bottom |
| Title | 0.6 | 0.45 | 12.13 | 0.95 (one or two lines at 27–32 pt) |
| Content | 0.6 | 1.6 | 12.13 | 5.2 |
| Caption and citation | inside the content zone, directly under the element they describe | | | 0.3–0.5 |

- 12 columns of 0.78 in with 0.25 in gutters across the 12.13 in content width.
- Align every element's edges to column edges and to the content zone's top; align text baselines across columns.
- Leave space empty rather than filling it; nothing within the margins.
- Slide numbers are optional; if used, small, muted, and in the same corner on every slide. No other repeated furniture.

## 1. Full-width figure

For one figure that carries the slide: a map, a time series, a simulation output.

- Figure across all 12 columns, as tall as the content zone allows after the caption (up to about 4.7 in).
- Caption and citation in one or two lines under it, left-aligned with the figure.
- Optionally one sentence of interpretation above the figure, or a conclusion title instead.

## 2. Figure with explanatory text

For a figure that needs a few sentences of reading guidance.

- Figure on 7–8 columns, text on the remaining 4–5, separated by one gutter; the figure on the side the eye reaches first
  (usually left).
- Text: two to four short statements or a short paragraph at body size; each statement points at something visible in
  the figure.
- Top edges aligned; caption under the figure only.

## 3. Two-figure comparison

For two cases, scenarios, or methods shown the same way.

- Two figures on 6 columns each, identical size, extent, axes, scales, and colour scale.
- Panel labels (a) and (b) with a short name each; one shared legend, placed once.
- One caption under both, or one under each if the sources differ.
- If the point is the difference, consider a third, difference figure on its own slide instead of a third panel.

## 4. Modelling workflow

For a model's structure: inputs, parameters, states, calculations, outputs.

- The diagram across all 12 columns, reading left to right or top to bottom, nodes on the column grid.
- A small key, once, explaining the node kinds (for example: observed input, parameter, state, calculation, output) by
  shape or line style.
- Connectors only where a quantity flows or is computed from another; label a connector only when the quantity is not
  obvious.
- Optionally one line under the diagram stating what the model computes.

## 5. Equation with definitions

For one to three related equations.

- Equations on 7 columns at the left, aligned on the relation sign, numbered if referred to elsewhere.
- Definitions on the remaining 5 columns as an aligned list: symbol, meaning, unit.
- Interpretation in words under the equations, separated from the definitions; no box around the equations.

## 6. Editable table

For values the audience needs to compare.

- A native table across up to 12 columns; at body size, about 8 rows by 6 columns at most. Split or move detail to an
  appendix slide beyond that.
- Light rules: top, under the header, bottom; no vertical rules; no zebra fills.
- Units in headers, consistent precision per column, numbers aligned on the decimal point; highlight only the few
  analytically important values (bold or the accent colour, not a fill).
- Source under the table.

## 7. Results synthesis

For the closing summary of findings.

- A conclusion title.
- Two to four findings as rows of text separated by light rules (not cards): each with the finding, its key number and
  unit, and a reference to the slide, figure, or citation that supports it.
- Optionally one small, real figure from the deck that anchors the main finding.

## Other slides

- **Title slide:** title (36–44 pt), presenter, affiliation, date or venue, aligned to the grid; nothing else, unless an
  authentic research image belongs there.
- **Section divider (optional):** only for talks with distinct parts; the section name on an otherwise empty slide.
- **References:** full references in a consistent style, at citation size or larger, in one or two columns.
- **Appendix:** the same layouts; label it once with the appendix title, not on every slide.
